## A brand a team of nine can run

Tessellate had eleven people and no designer. Any identity that needed a designer to
apply it was going to drift within a quarter — so the brief became: make something they
can hold on to without me.

## Scoping down on purpose

Most identity work fails here. A hundred-page guideline reads as thorough and behaves as
a filing cabinet nobody opens. We agreed up front on four pages, and everything that did
not earn its place on those pages did not get made.

- **One mark, two lockups.** Horizontal and stacked. No app icon variant until there was
  an app.
- **One typeface, five sizes.** Named for their job — `display`, `title`, `body`,
  `caption`, `micro` — not for their pixel values.
- **Four colours, with the contrast already checked.** Each pair in the palette is
  documented with the ratio it passes at.

## The rules that mattered

The guideline spends more words on what not to do than on what to do, because that is
where a small team loses coherence. Do not outline the mark. Do not set display type
below 24px. Do not introduce a fifth colour for a single campaign.

## Handover

A shared file, an asset kit with every export already sized, and the four pages as a PDF
pinned in their team channel. Six months on, nothing had drifted.
